Expanded benchmark findings relevant to ReproNim/reproseed#9 and #11
Updated October 5, 2026

Current PR context
ReproNim/reproseed#9 at da85ebdbf4cdbe472d6eb66c17f83bc9ea66f0c4 now
preserves every caller-set CPU variable, including empty values. The
original overwrite problem is resolved. Its NumPy policy preserves the
caller's list verbatim; the merge proposed in #11 is no longer needed
under that policy. #11 remains at 51d4c357 and conflicts with #9.

The remaining decision is whether MKL should default to AVX2 or COMPATIBLE.
#9 keeps AVX2 and documents explicit COMPATIBLE for mixed Intel/AMD use.
The October 5 review of #11 also identifies useful documentation on MKL
dynamic/thread controls, strict CNR, the limits of reproducibility, and
oneDNN's ISA cap (unless built with ONEDNN_ENABLE_MAX_CPU_ISA=OFF).

Evidence relevant to that decision
The October 4 study used 192 native processes on Typhon and Smaug (Intel)
and Unity cpu033 (AMD EPYC 7543), at 1 and 4 threads. COMPATIBLE matched
all nine tested outputs across hosts at each fixed thread count. The
AVX2 profile differed between Intel and AMD for three MKL GEMMs and
PyTorch exp and conv3d. Explicit AVX2 reproduced the old AVX2 profile.

COMPATIBLE time / AVX2-profile time, at 1 / 4 threads:
Host      MKL 1024-square   MKL 256x4096x256   PyTorch 1024-square matmul
Typhon    4.42 / 4.44       4.38 / 4.73        3.85 / 3.73
Smaug     4.60 / 4.35       4.30 / 4.39        3.79 / 3.40
Unity     4.37 / 4.19       3.89 / 3.89        3.32 / 3.08

COMPATIBLE still differed across thread counts for the skinny MKL GEMM.
AVX2,STRICT kept the tested Intel MKL GEMMs consistent across threads;
on the two larger Intel GEMMs its time was about 0.97-1.12x the AVX2
profile. It did not establish Intel/AMD agreement. The tested oneDNN
conv2d timing was essentially unchanged by COMPATIBLE.

Recommendation
Choose COMPATIBLE if the default's intended goal is agreement across
Intel and AMD at fixed thread counts and the measured cost is acceptable.
Keeping AVX2 with an explicit COMPATIBLE option is defensible when
throughput takes priority. The evidence does not determine that policy
choice or guarantee arbitrary workflow reproducibility. For #11, retain
only the useful documentation caveats identified in the review; its
original override/NumPy code changes have been superseded.

Scope and evidence
These measurements use #9 at 5f8229787001a946e4e020b68805661272577428,
#11 at 51d4c3578fe3bb43aeae87a29c5768a2cc53c4fd, and the recorded local
COMPATIBLE-default variant. They are NOT measurements of current #9.
No benchmarks were rerun for this publication. Timings exclude startup
and input generation; shared-host noise affects some small/four-thread
cases and conv3d. This is operator coverage, not end-to-end workflow,
GPU, training, or mixed-precision coverage. Bitwise differences do not
show which result is more accurate.
